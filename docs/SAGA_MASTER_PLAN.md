# Alienmon Saga Master Plan: Books 1–5

Oct 12, 2026 · @Sasquatch

The Alienmon Chronicles is one story told in five books: a boy follows a lost scout across fourteen worlds, stops a grieving man from tearing the sky open, and finally travels to the dying world the Alienmon fled to heal the thing that destroyed it. Every book follows the game's story in order and goes deeper than the game can. Two anime seasons cover the same ground.

## The saga at a glance

Logline. Rook, a thirteen-year-old from a canyon town, is chosen by Zib, a palm-sized scout carrying a message from the Seedmother of Ystra. To deliver it he must win eight Signal Shards, rescue his rival from a corporation built on a stolen secret, seal a tear in space, and cross through it to heal Oblivane, the starving guardian that ate Ystra's sky.

The shape of the saga. Books 1 and 2 are the journey and the mystery. Book 3 is the war and the first climax. Book 4 is home, truth and inheritance. Book 5 is the voyage to Ystra and the true ending. Each book ends on a hook that opens the next one.

Length target per book. 26 chapters, one per anime episode. Full Edition chapters run 2,500 to 3,000 words (about 70,000 words a book). Each book also gets a Quick Edition of 26 short chapters for the audiobook and young readers, as Book 1 has.

Story clock. Book 1 covers about two months. Book 2 covers about six weeks. Book 3 covers nine days. Book 4 opens one month later and covers a season. Book 5 covers about three weeks on Ystra, plus an epilogue one year later.

## Series bible

Every book must follow these facts and rules. Where the book and the game disagree, the books follow the canon listed here and the game is updated to match.

### The lore in brief

Ystra was a crystal world under a dying crimson sun. Its people, the Ystrans, listened to their world through the Signal, a song between minds.

The Seedmother is Ystra's living heart, a world-tree of crystal. Every Alienmon species began as one of her dreams.

The Three Pillars guard her: Terravox (land), Aquelion (seas and dreams) and Ignarch (the sun's warmth).

Oblivane was the fourth guardian, the moon-spirit who took in what was dying and returned it as starlight. When the sun failed, it took in too much and could not let go. Its hunger ate Ystra's sky. It is not evil; it is starving and lost. It is the Seedmother's sibling.

The Exodus. The Seedmother poured her strength into thousands of seedpods and sent them through a fold in space to the Kessler system. The Ystrans retuned eight ancient Relay Towers into a ring-shaped seal to hold the fold shut behind them. Each tower is anchored by a Signal Shard that only answers a true Bond.

Relay Gates are Ystran walkways between worlds (the Forge Gate, the harbor gate, the bridge under Dustmere). They wake for Zib and for anyone carrying Shards.

The Starfall happened 20 years before Book 1. The largest pod, the Mother Pod, struck the Starfall crater on Dustara, where the fold is thinnest. That weak spot is the Rift.

The Crater Four reached the Mother Pod first: Imogen Vashti, Teo Reyes, Kael Thorne and Orra Venn. They found a hatchling Zib and the Ystran Codex, whose last warning reads "Never open the door."

The crater pulse is the seal straining. Helix experiments have been cracking the towers for fifteen years.

### Running gags and motifs

Zib speaks only in "Bip." His lights show his mood: gold happy, pink pleased, purple grumpy, blue nervous, dim sad, every color at once overjoyed.

Plazmo's spark sneezes scare Zib into Rook's collar. As Solarach in Book 3, the sneeze becomes a tiny solar flare, and Zib still dives for cover.

DX-4 speaks in ALL CAPS, measures everything, and ends with "PROBABLY." Nana Wren built him; Book 4 reveals why.

Strand and Pell: "Helix Directive! Field Division! ...Second Class!" The joke grows up: Strand defects in Book 3, and Pell says the line alone in Book 4, then quietly adds "...Former."

"Think first, then act" is Rook's lesson from Book 1. Each book tests a new version of it.

The Spore Choir's song ("hmm-mm-mmm") from Book 1, Ch 11, becomes the melody of the Signal itself in Book 5.

"Everybody's somebody's somebody" is Nana Wren's motto. It is the last line Rook thinks before he reaches out to Oblivane.

### Tone and writing rules

Readers are ages 7 to 10. Language is simple and vivid; sentences mostly under 20 words.

Every chapter has one strong sensory set piece, one joke, one real feeling, and ends on a hook.

Battles are puzzles solved by thinking, teamwork and type matchups from the game's chart, never by shouting harder.

Nobody dies on the page. Danger is real, but loss is shown through grief that already happened (Elena, Rook's dad, Nana Wren).

Villains have reasons. Every reveal of something dark is answered by an act of friendship within the same book.

Point of view stays close on Rook, with short cutaways to Sola, Thorne or Vashti at the ends of chapters, as in Book 1.

## Book 2: The Eighth Tower

Rook races Helix for the last four Shards, rescues Sola from Helix Station, and wins the eighth Shard knowing it is the exact key Thorne needs to open the Rift. Game Chapters 9 to 14; anime Season 2, Episodes 27 to 39.

Theme: the truth is heavy, and friends carry it together.

Rook's "think first" test: a choice with no safe answer. Winning Shard 8 helps Thorne; not winning it lets the seal fail anyway.

Stakes clock: Thorne's "Phase Three begins when the eighth tower cracks." Every Shard Rook wins is a tower Helix can no longer crack, until the last one.

New faces: Old Basalt, Ingrid Vale, Grub, Admin Bronn, Echo and Iris, Mortimer Kade, Liss (full story), a Lumivex hatchling.

Zib memories: Memory 4 (Oblivane swallowing Ystra's sky) in Glacia; Memory 5 (the full message) in Umbra.

### Chapter outline

#### Part One: Fire and Ice (Pyros and Glacia)

1. Phase Three. The Neonis sky flickers as every tower pulses at once. Vashti shows Rook and Kit the two Codex pages she kept hidden. A new Relay Gate deep in Dustara's Magma Mines wakes for Zib. Hook: through it, a world that smells of sulfur.

2. Cinder Hollow. Pyros is miners, rail carts and red skies. Old Basalt, ancient and soot- black, refuses to battle until he sees who they are. Rook shows him Nana Wren's badge. Basalt says, "I knew a man who wore a jacket too big for him."

3. The Man Who Walked Into the Light. Basalt tells how Teo Reyes stood at the crater's edge six years ago, smiling, saying he could hear a song. He gives them Teo's old mining lantern. Rook catches Ashpup, a sooty pup that keeps the lantern lit.

4. The Deep Mines. Three levels of lava rails and collapsing tunnels. Kit fixes a runaway cart; Ashpup sniffs out a hidden ledge. Behind it is Teo's first cache: a recording, "If you find this, Sola, I'm not lost. I'm following them home."

5. Show Me Your Fire. Keeper battle at the Volcano Tower. Basalt's Scorchwyrm is too hot for Plazmet to hurt. Rook wins with Cactling and Lumislug grinding it down while Plazmet shields. Shard 5. Rook realizes Sola needs that recording more than anyone.

6. Frostmark. Glacia is aurora skies and ice-fishing towns. Ingrid Vale is stern and secretive. Her lab is locked, and Helix ice-cutters are drilling the glacier. Kit catches Iceburr, a prickly snowball, by fixing its frozen leg.

7. Aurora Route. A blizzard traps the team in a cave. Cold and scared, Kit admits she's afraid she'll be left behind once the "important" adventure starts. Rook answers by giving Kit the job of keeper of the Shards. Cactling evolves into Spinelord, holding the cave mouth against the storm.

8. Crystal Caverns. Three floors of mirror ice. Teo's second cache holds a crystal map: "the floating gardens beyond the light." Pell and Strand are here too, lost. Strand quietly tells Rook which way Helix is digging.

9. Teeth in the Ice. Ingrid finally opens her lab: fossils of Ystran creatures, bitten through by a mouth the size of a moon. She's been afraid to publish. The Devourer is real, and Rook feels sick: it's what the seal holds back.

10. Aurora Tower. Keeper battle against Ingrid's Shatterhorn (Cryo and Sonic) on cracking ice. Little Ashpup's Magma heat is the answer, and it melts the ice under Shatterhorn's hooves. Shard 6. Zib's Memory 4 plays: a shadow swallowing Ystra's sky. Then every world shakes in a pulse tremor. On Helix Station, Thorne smiles.

#### Part Two: Behind Helix Walls (Forge Fortress and Helix Station)

11. Grub's Confession. At Port Ember a junk trader, Grub, admits he designed Helix's capture cages and can't sleep for it. He knows the way into the Forge Fortress.

12. Infiltrate the Forge. Disguises, a laundry cart and Glitchbug scrambling cameras. In the Cage Hall, the team frees its first caged Alienmon and steals a keycard.

13. Go, Before I Change My Mind. Admin Bronn catches Rook red-handed. He battles him for the guards' sake, then opens a door: "Go. Before I change my mind." A friend inside Helix.

14. Helix Station. The stolen shuttle docks at a silver wheel in space. Everything is white, cold and humming. Strand meets them in a supply closet and draws them a map on a napkin.

15. The Containment Wing. Rows of glowing cages. Among the captives is a Lumivex hatchling, a crystal creature that glows like Teo's lantern. Helix guards close in.

16. Fungaroth. Kit refuses to leave without the captives. Mycelith evolves into Fungaroth and roots the whole wing open while the captives flood out. Kit, shaking: "Nobody gets left."

17. The Girl in the Helix Uniform. Sola blocks the Observation Deck in a Helix uniform. Rival battle R4: Tidaloid against Plazmet. Rook wins, then plays her Teo's recording.

18. Acceptable Loss. The deck screen plays Thorne's private log: "Reyes was an acceptable loss." Then a second file about Elena, Thorne's wife, lost in a reactor accident, and his plan to use the Rift to undo it. Sola tears off her Helix patch.

19. Abyssor. Admin Vex attacks. Sola and Rook fight side by side in a double battle; Tidaloid evolves into Abyssor. Vex escapes in a pod. The Lumivex hatchling chooses Sola. On the way home, Sola sleeps against Rook's shoulder.

#### Part Three: Visions and the Dark (Halo and Umbra)

20. Twin Visions. Halo is sky bridges and floating gardens. The twins Echo and Iris show a vision: the Rift tearing, and inside the darkness a kind face no one else noticed.

21. Echoes of Liss. Liss leads them through three wind shrines and tells her story. She launched the last seedpod, the one carrying Zib, and stayed behind on purpose. Zib cries in lights.

22. Mirror Match. Keeper battle against the twins, who see every move one turn ahead. Rook wins by letting his team choose their own moves. Shard 7. Sola and Rook finally laugh together.

23. Gravewell. Umbra is a dark city of lantern-keepers and Helix refugees. The team relights the lanterns of Gravewell, and the people start trusting them.

24. The Sinkhole. Four levels down to Kade's ruined lab. Mortimer Kade built Thorne's Rift engine. His schematics show the truth: the eighth tower's resonance is the key that opens the Rift.

25. No Safe Answer. If Rook wins Shard 8, Thorne gets his key. If he doesn't, the cracked seal fails on its own. Rook, Sola and Kit choose together, then beat Kade at Gravewell Tower. Shard 8.

26. The Message. All eight Shards ring, and Zib's full message plays: the Seedmother is alive, and Oblivane is her lost sibling who can be healed. Then a Helix alarm: Thorne's fleet is over the Starfall crater. To be continued in Book 3: Phase Three.

## Book 3: Phase Three

In nine days, Thorne turns the whole Kessler system against the seal, Rook rallies every Keeper to hold it, and at the bottom of the Starfall crater the Rift opens, Oblivane looks through, and Rook's team sings it shut. Game Chapters 15 and 16, expanded with a system-wide siege the game only hints at; anime Season 2, Episodes 40 to 52.

Theme: grief can make you open doors you should leave shut; love is what closes them.

Rook's "think first" test: he can't win by battling. He has to lead other people and trust them.

Stakes clock: each chapter in Parts One and Two is one day. Day 9 is the descent.

Why it's bigger than the game: the game shows Ch 15 and 16 at the crater. The book adds the eight-world siege, so every Keeper from Books 1 and 2 returns and earns a moment.

New faces: General Ostrander, Novaryx, Thornvoid. Returning: Tamsin, Bronn, Kade, every Keeper.

### Chapter outline

#### Part One: The Pulse War (Days 1–4)

1. Over the Crater. Thorne's fleet hangs over the Starfall crater. He broadcasts to every world, calm and kind: the Rift will give endless free power, and the Linker League has been hiding it. Many people believe him.

2. The Bridge Won't Stop Humming. Rook brings Sola home to Dustmere. The bridge hums day and night. Mom feeds everyone. DX-4 reports the Rift growing "0.4 PERCENT PER HOUR. THIS IS BAD. DEFINITELY." For once he doesn't say "probably."

3. The Keepers' Call. Kit builds a Shard relay from Nana Wren's ship radio, so the eight Shards can talk to the eight towers. Through DJ Static's frequency, Rook calls every Keeper. One by one, they answer.

4. Hank's Iron Wall. Helix crews attack the Ferrox tower to crack it. Kit and Hank hold it together with Gleamgrub and every scrapper in Rustvale. Kit finally battles beside her dad, not behind him.

#### Part Two: Taking Back the Towers (Days 5–8)

5. Harbor Reclaimed. Captain Marsh and Rook raid a Helix freighter and win back Thalassa's stolen core, the one Vex took in Book 1. The harbor tower lights again. Marsh grins: "Now we're even."

6. Static Goes Live. DJ Static broadcasts Thorne's private logs, "acceptable loss" and all, to every world. The system turns against Helix. Thorne switches his screen off and keeps working.

7. Two Old Friends. Vashti calls Thorne on a Helix channel. Flashback to the Crater Four night: four teenagers, a glowing pod, a sleeping Zib. Kael was the one who carried it out. He refuses her. "You hid a door, Imogen. I'm opening it."

8. Elena. A cutaway chapter in Thorne's point of view: the reactor, the lost minute, the eight years of trying to get it back. Readers should understand him without agreeing with him.

9. Strand Defects. Strand steals Pell's shuttle and lands it in Dustmere's plaza. "Field Division. Second Class. Resigning." He'll fly them past the military cordon.

10. Rim Camp. General Ostrander's soldiers have guarded the crater for twenty years and won't let children through. Tamsin, the explorer from Book 1, has mapped every inch of the crater. She becomes their guide.

11. Seedpod Fields. A valley of cracked, glowing seedpod shells. Zib finds his own pod, the last one Liss launched. He sits in it for a long, quiet minute.

12. Teo's Old Camp. The third cache: Teo's journal. Sola reads it aloud by lantern light, the first time she cries in front of Rook without hiding it. The last page is a drawing of her at seven.

13. Eight Shards Sing. At the crater rim, the Shards rise into a ring and Novaryx, a star- creature of the Rift, answers. Rook asks it to guide them instead of catching it. It agrees.

14. Pell's Choice. Vex orders Pell to net Zib at the rim. Pell freezes, then throws the net gun into the crater. Ashpup evolves into Cinderhound to hold off Vex's Vexhydra while everyone escapes down.

#### Part Three: The Descent (Day 9)

15. Crater Descent. Three levels of glowing rock, falling crystal and Helix drones. Novaryx lights the way. Kit disarms a gravity trap with Iceburr.

16. Vex Again. Vex's rematch on the second level. Rook and Sola fight together again, Plazmet and Abyssor in perfect sync. Vex escapes once more.

17. The Back Door. Kade's schematics show a maintenance path into the engine. Admin Bronn, still undercover, is waiting at the end of it. He shuts down half of Thorne's guards.

18. The Engine Platform. A ring of machines around a crack in the air that hums like the Dustmere bridge. Thorne stands at the controls with Thornvoid beside him.

19. The Door Opens. Thorne turns the key, the eighth Shard's resonance copied from Rook's own Linker. The Rift tears open. On every world, people look up and see an eye in the sky.

20. Director Thorne. The final battle. Thornvoid is Gravity and Void, and it shrugs off almost everything Rook has. Thinking first, Rook calls in his friends: Sola's Squawktone (Sonic beats Void) and Kit's Iceburr (Cryo beats Gravity) strike while Plazmet shields them. Rook wins by leading, not by hitting harder.

21. What Thorne Saw. Thorne looks into the Rift for Elena and sees only Oblivane, starving and enormous and sad. Nothing on the other side can give her back. He drops to his knees.

22. Zib Fading. The Rift pulls at everything. Zib pours his light into the Shards to hold the ring together, and his lights start going out one by one.

23. Sing the Signal. Rook, Sola, Novaryx and Zib sing the Spore Choir's melody. Over Static's broadcast, every Keeper and every Alienmon on every world sings with them. Thorne reroutes the engine to strengthen the seal.

24. Solarach. Plazmet blazes into Solarach, a small floating star with three plasma rings, and pushes the Rift closed with light.

25. The Last Light. In the closing crack, a man steps through with a crystal Alienmon at his side: Teo Reyes and his Lumivex. Sola runs. The Rift seals.

26. From the Other Side. Zib's lights come back, slowly. Ostrander arrests Thorne, who asks only to say goodbye to Vashti. Teo tells them the truth: the seal can only be truly healed from Ystra's side, and the Seedmother is fading. To be continued in Book 4: The Summit.

## Book 4: The Summit

Back home, Rook learns why Zib chose him, watches the grown-ups finally tell the truth, passes the Prime Linker's test, and opens the Vortex to Ystra. Game Chapters 17 and 18, plus the postgame extras the game saves for later (Vex's capture, Saga's secret, the Quorum's history); anime Season 3, Episodes 53 to 65.

Theme: secrets kept to protect people end up hurting them; carry the truth in the open.

Rook's "think first" test: the hardest battle is one he shouldn't try to win.

Tone shift: the quietest book. More home, more family, more laughter, and a slow build to the biggest decision of the saga.

Payoffs from Book 1: Dusty finally names the Crater Four; Nana Wren's badge, journal and starship; DX-4's origin; Tamsin's question-mark patch is set up for Book 5.

New faces: the Quorum (Marisol Fenn, Duke Harrow, Saga, General Ostrander as a Quorum member), Orra Venn.

### Chapter outline

#### Part One: Homecoming (Dustmere)

1. Next Door. One month later. Teo and Sola move into the empty house next to Rook's. Teo sleeps most of the day; Lumivex keeps watch on the roof. Sola brings Mom a cactus-berry pie, badly burned, and Mom says it's perfect.

2. The Starfall Festival. Dustmere celebrates the twentieth anniversary of the Starfall. The bridge glows on its own. At the bonfire, Dusty finally tells the end of his story and names the four kids. Everybody looks at Teo.

3. Six Years Beyond the Light. Teo tells what he found: floating crystal gardens, a dying red sun, Ystran echoes who taught him to listen, and a Lumivex that kept him alive. He saw the Seedmother once, far away, growing dimmer every year.

4. The Attic. Rook finds Nana Wren's journal in the Recovery Bay attic. The night of the Starfall, she nursed three pods of baby saucers like Zib, and she heard a voice singing. She sang back. Zib chose Rook because the same Signal runs in his family.

5. DX-4's Secret. The journal's last page explains DX-4: Nana built him to listen for the scout's frequency and watch over her grandchild. "Probably" was her own favorite word. DX-4 is quiet for a long time, then says, "I KNEW THAT. PROBABLY."

6. An Ordinary Day. Rook and Sola spend one whole day being thirteen: a dust-bike race, a swim in the canyon pool, a contest to make Plazmo and Zib laugh. Sola admits it's the first normal day she's had since she was seven.

#### Part Two: The Reckoning

7. The Trial. In a Neonis courtroom, Strand testifies with shaking hands. Vashti testifies too and confesses her own twenty years of silence in public. Thorne pleads guilty to everything and looks at no one.

8. A Letter From a Cell. Thorne's first letter to Rook includes a Codex page he copied twenty years ago, one Orra and Vashti never read. It describes a "door that is a road," opened only by a scout and a listener together.

9. Captain Marsh's Revenge. Vex is hiding aboard a smuggler's freighter off Thalassa. Rook, Kit, Marsh and Pell catch her together. Pell stands on the deck and announces, "Helix Directive! Field Division! ...Former."

10. The Invitation. A message comes from Apex Array, from Orra Venn, the Prime Linker and last of the Crater Four: "Come to the Summit." Vashti reads it twice and admits she and Orra haven't spoken in fifteen years.

11. Kit's Lens. Kit and Vashti work out the Codex page: Apex Array's Great Telescope can focus all eight Shards into a Vortex, if someone builds a lens from Teo's crystal map. Kit starts building.

#### Part Three: The Summit (Apex Array)

12. The Summit Lift. A glass lift climbs a mountain above the clouds. Sola challenges Rook to a friendly rival match, R5, just for fun. She wins, barely, and catches an Astrowl at the top.

13. The Hall of Crystal. Marisol Fenn, a collector, battles with Facetrix. She shows the Ystran crystal that cut the first Shard and tells how the Crater Four built the first Linker.

14. The Hall of Sound. Duke Harrow, an aging rock legend, battles with Encorex. He wrote the anthem played at the Linker League's founding, and he tells how Orra and Vashti sealed the Codex away.

15. The Hall of Fire. Saga, the silent hooded master, battles without a word. As Saga's last Alienmon falls, Saga whispers one word in Ystran. Zib's lights flash every color at once. Nobody else noticed.

16. The Hall of Gravity. General Ostrander guarded the crater for twenty years. He confesses he saw Teo walk into the light and let him go, because Teo looked happy.

17. The Prime Linker. Orra Venn is old, upright and kind. She takes Rook to the Great Telescope and shows him the sealed Codex.

18. Could You Carry What I Carried? Orra battles with a Plasma ace far stronger than anything Rook has faced. When her final attack would hurt Zib, Rook stops the battle and shields him. Orra lowers her hand. That was the test.

19. In the Open. Orra and Vashti, side by side, broadcast the whole truth to every world: the Codex, the seal, Oblivane, their silence. Then Orra names Rook the next keeper of the secret, to carry it in the open.

20. Who Goes? Teo explains: the seal can only be healed from Ystra, and the Seedmother has months, not years. The grown-ups plan to go alone. Zib refuses to move unless Rook comes.

21. Mom. Rook asks Mom. She cries, then tells him about his dad holding him up to the window: "This one's going to see all of it." She says yes, and pins Nana Wren's badge back on his jacket herself.

22. Goodbyes. Hank gives Kit a Gearhound and his old welding goggles. The Keepers gather at Apex. Strand promises to keep the Dustmere Recovery Bay running. DX-4 asks to be told "EVERYTHING. IN ORDER."

23. Tamsin's Patch. Tamsin asks to come, pointing at the question mark on her vest. "Forty years I've looked for that world. I'd like to see it before I'm done."

24. Aligning the Telescope. Kit's lens is fitted and the Shards are set in a ring, but nothing happens. Then Rook remembers the Codex: a scout and a listener together. He and Zib hum the Spore Choir song.

25. The Vortex. The sky above the telescope folds open into a spinning road of light. Vashti will stay to hold it open from the Kessler side. A last message from Thorne arrives: "Bring her home, all of her."

26. Through. Rook, Kit, Sola, Teo, Tamsin and their partners step into the Vortex. They come out under a huge, tired red sun, in a forest of crystal trees that chime in the wind: the forest from Rook's very first dream. To be continued in Book 5: The Seedmother's Song.

## Book 5: The Seedmother's Song

On dying Ystra, Rook earns the trust of the Three Pillars, gathers the scattered Ystran echoes into a Choir, and reaches Oblivane not as an enemy but as someone lost, healing it, waking the Seedmother, and joining two worlds into one home. The game's postgame, "Through the Vortex"; anime Season 3, Episodes 66 to 78. This is the saga's true ending.

Theme: hunger isn't evil. Everybody's somebody's somebody.

Rook's "think first" test: the final one. The strongest move against Oblivane is to stop attacking and listen.

Structure: a quest through Ystra's eight regions in the game's order. Each region tests one person on the team, and each test pays off something from an earlier book.

Stakes clock: the Vortex weakens every day Vashti holds it open alone. They have about three weeks.

Oblivane's typing: Void and Gravity. Sonic and Cryo moves hurt it most (Squawktone, Lumivex, Iceburr), but the battle can only calm it, never defeat it.

Ending: the shared-home choice. Ystra is renewed, the Warp Gate links it to Kessler, and Alienmon may live on either world.

### Chapter outline

#### Part One: The Pillars (Arrival Fragment to the Sun Forge)

1. The Crystal Forest. They arrive in the forest from Rook's dream. The sun is enormous, red and tired. Half the sky is simply missing, a black hole where stars should be. Zib flies in joyful circles; this is home.

2. Echoes. Ystran echoes flicker between the trees like shy ghosts. Liss arrives, fully herself for the first time, and greets Teo as an old friend. She explains the plan: the Pillars, the Choir, then Oblivane.

3. The Floating Continents. Islands of land drift in the sky, held up by Terravox. Kit has to rebuild a broken sky-bridge with Gearhound and Gleamgrub so the team can cross.

4. Terravox Tests Strength. Terravox carries a forest on its back and will not listen to the weak. Its test isn't power: it asks Spinelord, Rook's old grumpy Cactling, to hold up one falling island. Spinelord does, with every friend pushing beside it.

5. The Drowned Seas. A sunken Ystran city under glowing water. Abyssor carries Sola down. Sola dreams of her father at seven and wakes crying underwater.

6. Aquelion Tests Memory. Aquelion keeps the dreams of the sleeping. It shows each of them their saddest memory and asks if they would erase it. Sola keeps hers, the night her dad left, because it's also the night he kissed her goodnight.

7. The Sun Forge. A furnace at the top of the world where Ignarch feeds the dying sun. The heat is unbearable. Solarach is the only one who can go near it.

8. Ignarch Tests Courage. Ignarch asks Rook to step into the Forge's light without his partners. He's terrified. He goes anyway, alone, and inside the light finds Nana Wren's voice singing the Signal. The Pillars agree to help.

#### Part Two: The Choir (Crystal Ruins to the Blighted Forest)

9. Saga. A hooded figure has followed them through the Vortex: Saga, the silent Quorum master. Saga lowers the hood. Saga is a Ystran echo who came in the Mother Pod and has lived twenty years in a borrowed human shape, guarding the Vortex secret. Saga is Liss's brother.

10. The Crystal Ruins. The great Choir chamber where all Ystrans once sang together is shattered. Kit realizes it's an instrument, not a building, and starts to fix it.

11. Gathering the Echoes. The team hunts for scattered echoes across the ruins. Each one is shy and frightened. They call them with the Spore Choir song from Book 1.

12. Tamsin's World. Tamsin finds the ruined map room of the Ystran explorers. On the wall is a symbol shaped exactly like a question mark: the Ystran word for "the place we haven't found yet." She sits down and laughs until she cries.

13. The Choir Wakes. Kit's repairs hold. The echoes gather and sing for the first time in two hundred years. The ruins light up, and across the Vortex, Vashti hears it on Kessler and holds on.

14. The Blighted Forest. Where Oblivane has fed, the forest is gray and silent. Fungaroth can heal the blight, slowly, tree by tree. Kit and Fungaroth stay behind to cleanse it while the others go on.

15. The Hungry Things. Small shadow-creatures, pieces of Oblivane's hunger, swarm the travelers. Glitchbug and Cinderhound fight them off. Rook notices they aren't attacking. They're begging.

16. The Vortex Flickers. A message from Vashti: she can hold the road only nine more days. Teo wants to turn back. Sola, for the first time, overrules her father.

#### Part Three: The Song (the Edge of the Void to the Seedmother's Grove)

17. The Edge of the Void. The ground simply stops. Beyond it is a darkness so complete it has no shape. Something enormous is breathing inside it.

18. Oblivane. It rises: a moon-sized shadow with one vast, tired eye, the eye from the end of Book 1. It reaches for the Choir's light because it's starving. The Pillars and every partner fight to hold it back.

19. The Battle That Can't Be Won. Squawktone, Lumivex and Iceburr hurt it, and every hit makes it hungrier. Solarach is nearly swallowed. Rook stops the battle.

20. Somebody's Somebody. Rook walks to the edge alone with Zib. He thinks of Nana Wren's words and talks to Oblivane the way she talked to a frightened pod twenty years ago. He asks what it lost.

21. What Oblivane Remembers. Oblivane answers in the Signal: it remembers being the moon-spirit who carried the dying home as starlight. It never wanted to keep them. It just couldn't let go.

22. Let Go. The Choir sings, the Pillars sing, Sola, Kit, Teo and Tamsin sing, and Zib plays the Seedmother's message into the dark. Oblivane lets go. Two hundred years of swallowed light pour back into Ystra's sky as starlight.

23. The Seedmother's Grove. At the heart of the world, the Seedmother wakes, renewed. She calls Oblivane her sibling and holds it, now small and silver, like a moon in her branches.

24. The Choice. The Seedmother offers two futures: rebuild Ystra as the Alienmon's home, or keep Kessler as their home. Rook asks for a third: both. The Warp Gate wakes, a permanent road between the worlds.

25. Zib's Choice. The Seedmother tells Zib his mission is done; he can stay home. Zib looks at her, then at Rook, and says one long, soft "Bip." The Seedmother smiles: "A scout's job is to find a home. You found one." Zib comes back with Rook.

26. One Year Later. Rook is fourteen. The Warp Gate hums over Dustmere. Tamsin stayed on Ystra to explore; Teo and Vashti run a new research station between the worlds; Vex is still in prison, and Marsh still visits to gloat. A letter arrives from Thorne: "Elena would have liked you. Tell your Zib thank you." At the Observatory, a nervous new thirteen- year-old chooses a starter, and Rook notices one shy capsule nobody picked. He walks over to it. The end of the Alienmon Chronicles.

### Open questions to settle before writing

Teo's years missing. The books say six; the game bible says three. This plan uses six. Update the game's Story Bible and dialogue to match.

Lumislug's type. Book 1's Xenodex lists Lumislug as Bio, but the game data says Toxin. Fix the Xenodex in Book 1, or change Lumislug's type in the game.

Kit in the game. Kit is a book and anime original. Decide whether to add Kit to the game as a companion NPC from Ferrox onward; it would strengthen the tie between all three.

Saga as Liss's brother. This is new canon in this plan. The game only says Saga is a Ystran echo in a human body, so this fits, but confirm it before Book 4 is written.

Batch 2 tease. Book 5 ends the saga cleanly. If a second saga is planned, add one line to the epilogue, for example a new signal from beyond the Warp Gate.
